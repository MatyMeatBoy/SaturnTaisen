# Chapter 1 park speech performance

The owner reported slow sound and a flashing background as Sakura begins speaking on another PC with v0.2.7-alpha. The local replay reaches the same park conversation from a recorded Chapter 1 checkpoint. The shipped executable was tested separately. It predates the BG1207 HD substitution and showed several slow frames while the new mouth poses were first matched. The three late matching frames took 27–28 ms each. The emulator's sound work on those frames took about 1.2 ms.

Patch 0154 reuses a mixed floral texture when native input pixels, effective blend recipes, backdrop and placement match. Resize and renderer resets invalidate the relevant entries. Patch 0155 samples each candidate mouth or eye mask once and scores the same 49 shifted windows with bit counts for narrow strips. Both patches run through the shared Enhanced presentation path.

The combined Direct3D11 2560×1440 A/B used 400 frames, a hidden window and a dummy SDL audio device. It compared the candidate with the same scene and settings on the unoptimized patch 0152 executable. Average backdrop and composition time fell from 3.631 to 1.833 ms. Frames over 16.666 ms fell from seven to three in that run. The three late mouth frames fell below the threshold. The first new-pose frame fell from 41.27 to 24.20 ms. The cache reused 743 draws and uploaded 159 textures. All guest hashes, seven face-match decisions and eight screenshots matched exactly. Strict execution reported zero interpreted blocks. A separate cache A/B verified exact 1440p and 1080p resize captures.

A separate 240-frame 1440p A/B dropped average composition from 4.412 to 1.901 ms and its 95th percentile from 5.635 to 2.361 ms. Both runs loaded the floral asset once and recorded the same layout changes. The reported flicker and audio behavior on the other PC still need confirmation. The capture and replay inputs are private under `local/work/codex-perf-park`.

The release took no game assets. The workspace measured 73,591,870,421 logical bytes, above the 20 GB cap. The delivered development ZIP measured 6,315,009,692 bytes and was left intact.

## Guest audio startup

The frontend schedules NTSC guest fields at 59.8261 Hz and submits 44.1 kHz stereo PCM after each guest frame. The original startup resumed the SDL device while its stream was empty. The first guest batch reached the stream 55–96 ms after opening in two muted WASAPI park trials, and the interval to the second was 78–109 ms. The device requested 6 and 10 wholly empty buffers before the first batch. A separate dummy-device injection delayed one producer pass by 50 ms after startup and caused an empty pull. The injection exercises ongoing starvation independently of device startup.

Patch 0157 defers the initial SDL resume on the normal-speed realtime guest path until at least 5,880 input bytes are queued, about two guest fields. It resumes immediately for unthrottled playback and when the chooser, menu, pause or alternate speed intervenes. If a PC film supplies the first audio, its existing queue fill starts the device in the same pass. Once started, the device follows the existing audio transport and frame deadlines.

Two instrumented WASAPI comparisons used the same 400-frame 1440p park route with VDP2 line reuse enabled. The gate resumed after guest field 2 with 5,904 bytes queued in both runs. Before the first guest PCM, the queued start had no empty requests against 6 and 10 on immediate start. In the first 500 ms following the first PCM push there were 3 and 0 wholly empty requests against 7 and 16. The SDL callback measures input shortfall and wholly empty available output. The threshold corresponds to 33 ms of audio, while waiting for the second producer batch added 72–104 ms before device resume in these two runs. Outcomes after 500 ms varied with host timing. This startup change leaves later producer stalls and the reported slower-PC audio and flicker open.

All four instrumented park runs reproduced the same 400 strict guest hashes and submitted 1,180,628 PCM bytes without queue submission errors. The dummy tests covered unthrottled launch, interruption while priming, and MOVIE000 with the first two guest audio batches suppressed by the probe. The uninstrumented builds agreed on 400 park hashes, 400 clip hashes and 425 hashes through menu, pause, slow speed and a development reset. Both switched to MOVIE000 at guest frame 3 and back to guest audio at frame 63. All of these comparisons reported zero interpreted blocks. These were muted device and guest-state measurements. Natural audio on the reported PC and inclusion in a release remain open.

The isolated trial retained 2,682,819 bytes of local evidence after deleting 575,357,440 bytes of regenerable caches. The project measured 70,842,878,238 logical bytes. The delivered development ZIP remains 6,315,009,692 bytes. The private receipts are under `local/work/codex-audio-pacing-probe`.

## HD body texture publication

Three 400-field real-time replays after patch 0157 used a muted WASAPI device and a monotonic trace at the SDL queue and frame boundaries. After the first 500 ms, the device made 20, 0 and 0 undersupplied requests. Two requests in the first run were wholly empty. They fell in 23–24.5 ms producer gaps that included 18–20 ms of guest work. The longest recurring gap, between fields 206 and 207, was 30.0–40.2 ms. Its previous frame spent 20–29 ms in composition and less than 0.3 ms in the SDL flip. The queue survived this gap in all three nominal runs. Injecting a separate 45 ms stall at field 207 provoked one empty pull. A 720p trial had no wholly empty steady pulls, and its largest gap included 46 ms of guest work. These observations do not establish a repeatable sustained underrun on the measured host.

The NC10 donor builds CPU data asynchronously. The render thread previously created and uploaded the body texture and all 93 face textures together. Patch 0159 keeps the decoded pixels and uploads each selected texture on first use, within the same draw. The lookups, fit and native fallback follow the same selections. The body texture cache is invalidated on renderer recreation and device reset, with CPU data available to rebuild selected textures.

Two matched unthrottled 1440p park A/B runs put all 94 eager uploads on field 206. They took 11.55 and 19.83 ms. The candidate uploaded the body on field 206 in 0.53 and 0.51 ms. It uploaded the only selected face texture on field 247 in 0.68 and 0.43 ms. The eager field 206 took 23.57 and 33.22 ms including the guest frame. Both candidate traces kept field 206 below the 16.666 ms slow-frame threshold. Loading and other steady-state timings varied across runs.

The cold visual comparisons held the async decode at its first demand in both builds to pin texture readiness to the same guest field. Twenty-one 1440p PNG pairs including fields 203–212 matched exactly, with the same eight logged face decisions. The same samples matched when the candidate received a synthetic SDL render-device reset at field 300 and recreated the needed texture. All 400 strict guest hashes matched and each run reported zero interpreted blocks. Four 1440p samples and 55 guest hashes each also matched for NC3018 and NC81, as did the 21 samples and 400 hashes with the NC10 donor absent. Physical device loss, audible audio on the reported PC, and release packaging remain open.
